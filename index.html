HOJA 5

READING · The first woman who studied chimpanzees

10 × 0.1 = 1 mark

On the morning of July 14, 1960, Jane Goodall arrived at Lake Tanganyika and the Gombe National Park in Africa. She had brought a tent, a couple of binoculars and her mother. A group of local men met the strange pair of women and helped them to carry their camping equipment. Then, around 5 p.m., somebody reported that they had seen a chimpanzee. Straight away, Jane went into the forest to find her first chimpanzee.

As a young woman, Jane Goodall had no scientific qualifications. However, this didn't stop her from following her childhood dream of studying chimpanzees in Africa. She wanted to study the animals and find out how they really lived. After many months of difficult work she made three important discoveries: chimpanzees ate meat, they used tools to get food and they also made tools. Every evening, Jane wrote her findings in a diary and she began to publish articles in journals such as National Geographic magazine. After a while, scientists and academics started reading her studies and Jane was offered a place at a university. After more years of research, she became Doctor Jane Goodall in 1966. Her work made her famous.

During the seventies, Gombe became a dangerous place to work. There was fighting between soldiers and rebels, but Jane stayed. Nowadays, she spends long time periods interviewing and lecturing, meeting with government officers as well as collecting money for the Jane Goodall Institute which continues her research.

Grammar Guide

Question formation

Present simple: Do/Does + subject + base verb? · Past simple: Did + subject + base verb? · Present perfect: Have/Has + subject + past participle? · Past continuous: Was/Were + subject + -ing?

Example: Why did you apply? NOT “Why did you applied?”

Passive voice

Present passive: am/is/are + past participle. Past passive: was/were + past participle. Questions: Was + subject + past participle?

Example: The film was directed by… / When was the airplane invented?

Verb tenses

Past simple = finished action. Past continuous = background in progress. Present perfect = past event connected to now. Present continuous = now. Will = prediction/promise. Going to = plan.

Example: She was studying when she met him. / She has studied English for 12 years.

Other exam grammar

First conditional: If + present simple, will + infinitive. Second conditional: If + past simple, would + infinitive. Should = advice. Told + object + not to + infinitive.

Example: If I study, I will pass. / I told them not to run.

Writing framework

Four paragraphs: 1 introduction; 2 personal life; 3 career and achievements; 4 why you admire this person. Include linking words.

Example: Firstly, In addition, However, For example, As a result, Finally.
